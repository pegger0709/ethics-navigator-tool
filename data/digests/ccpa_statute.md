The Act is the California Consumer Privacy Act of 2018, Civil Code sections 1798.100 through 1798.199.100, as amended and effective January 1, 2025.
The Act establishes the California Privacy Protection Agency, which is vested with full administrative power, authority, and jurisdiction to implement and enforce the Act and which is governed by a five-member board whose members are appointed by the Governor, the Attorney General, the Senate Rules Committee, and the Speaker of the Assembly and who shall have expertise in privacy, technology, and consumer rights.
A business that controls the collection of a consumer's personal information shall, at or before the point of collection, inform consumers of the categories of personal information to be collected, the purposes for which they are collected or used, and whether that information is sold or shared.
A business that collects sensitive personal information shall inform consumers at or before collection of the categories of sensitive personal information collected, the purposes of collection or use, and whether that information is sold or shared.
A business shall not collect additional categories of personal information or sensitive personal information, or use information collected for additional incompatible purposes, without providing the consumer with notice.
A business shall disclose the length of time it intends to retain each category of personal information, or the criteria used to determine that period, and shall not retain information for longer than is reasonably necessary for the disclosed purpose.
A business that controls the collection of personal information as a third party may satisfy its notice obligation by providing the required information prominently and conspicuously on the homepage of its website, and shall provide clear and conspicuous notice at the location where it collects information on its premises, including in a vehicle.
A business' collection, use, retention, and sharing of personal information shall be reasonably necessary and proportionate to achieve the disclosed purposes and shall not involve further processing incompatible with those purposes.
A business that sells or shares personal information to a third party or discloses it to a service provider or contractor shall enter into a written agreement limiting the purposes of the disclosure, obligating the recipient to provide the same level of privacy protection required by the Act, and granting the business rights to take reasonable steps to ensure the recipient uses the information consistently with the business' obligations.
A consumer shall have the right to request that a business delete personal information the business has collected about the consumer, and the business shall direct its service providers and contractors to delete the information accordingly.
A business is not required to comply with a deletion request where retaining the information is necessary to complete a transaction, to ensure security, to debug or repair errors, to exercise free speech, to comply with the California Electronic Communications Privacy Act, to engage in conduct public or peer-reviewed scientific research conducted with the consumer's informed consent where deletion would render the research impossible or seriously impair it, to enable solely internal uses reasonably aligned with the consumer's expectations, or to comply with a legal obligation.
A consumer shall have the right to request that a business that maintains inaccurate personal information about the consumer correct it, and the business shall use commercially reasonable efforts to correct the information as directed.
A consumer shall have the right to request that a business disclose the categories of personal information it has collected about the consumer, the categories of sources of the information, the business or commercial purpose for collecting, selling, or sharing it, the categories of third parties to whom it discloses the information, and the specific pieces of personal information it has collected about the consumer.
A consumer shall have the right to request that a business that sells or shares personal information, or discloses it for a business purpose, disclose the categories of personal information collected, sold, or shared, the categories of third parties to whom it was sold or shared, and the categories of persons to whom it was disclosed for a business purpose.
A third party shall not sell or share personal information that has been sold to or shared with the third party by a business unless the consumer has received explicit notice and an opportunity to exercise the right to opt out.
A consumer shall have the right at any time to direct a business that sells or shares personal information about the consumer to third parties not to sell or share the consumer's personal information.
A business that receives personal information as an asset in a merger, acquisition, bankruptcy, or other transaction shall comply with an opt-out direction the consumer previously gave to the transferor.
A business shall not sell or share the personal information of consumers under 16 years of age unless the consumer, in the case of consumers at least 13 and less than 16 years of age, or the consumer's parent or guardian, in the case of consumers under 13, has affirmatively authorized the sale or sharing.
A business that has received a consumer's opt-out direction shall not sell or share the consumer's personal information and shall wait at least 12 months before requesting that the consumer authorize the sale or sharing again.
A consumer shall have the right, at any time, to direct a business that uses or discloses sensitive personal information to limit that use or disclosure to the uses necessary to perform the services or provide the goods reasonably expected by an average consumer and the enumerated business purposes.
A business that has received a consumer's direction to limit sensitive personal information shall not use or disclose it for other purposes unless the consumer subsequently provides consent.
Sensitive personal information that is collected or processed without the purpose of inferring characteristics about a consumer is not subject to the right to limit and shall be treated as personal information under the Act.
A business shall not discriminate against a consumer for exercising rights under the Act, including by denying goods or services, charging different prices or rates, providing a different level or quality of goods or services, suggesting such differences, or retaliating against an employee, applicant, or independent contractor.
A business may offer financial incentives, including payments, and different prices, rates, levels, or quality of goods or services, if the difference is reasonably related to the value provided to the business by the consumer's data.
A business may enter a consumer into a financial incentive program only with the consumer's prior opt-in consent, which may be revoked at any time, and if the consumer refuses consent the business shall wait at least 12 months before requesting it again.
A business shall not use financial incentive practices that are unjust, unreasonable, coercive, or usurious in nature.
A business shall make available to consumers two or more designated methods for submitting rights requests, including at a minimum a toll-free telephone number, except that an exclusively online business need only provide an email address.
A business that maintains an internet website shall make the website available to consumers for submitting requests.
A business shall respond to a verifiable consumer request free of charge within 45 days of receipt, and may extend that period once by an additional 45 days when reasonably necessary, provided the consumer is given notice of the extension within the first 45 days.
Disclosures shall be made in writing, delivered through the consumer's account if one exists or by mail or electronically at the consumer's option, and in a readily useable format that allows the consumer to transmit the information to another entity without hindrance.
A business may require reasonable authentication of the consumer but shall not require the consumer to create an account to make a verifiable consumer request.
Disclosures required under the rights to know shall cover the 12-month period preceding the request, and a consumer may request information beyond that period unless doing so proves impossible or would involve a disproportionate effort, for personal information collected on or after January 1, 2022.
A service provider or contractor shall assist the business in responding to verifiable consumer requests but is not required to comply with requests received directly from a consumer for information it holds in its role as service provider or contractor.
A business shall disclose in its online privacy policy, and update at least once every 12 months, a description of consumers' rights and its request methods, lists of the categories of personal information it has collected, sold, or shared in the preceding 12 months, the categories of its sources, the purposes for collection, sale, or sharing, the categories of third parties to whom it discloses information, and the categories it has disclosed for a business purpose, or shall disclose that it has not sold, shared, or disclosed such information.
A business is not obligated to provide information disclosures to the same consumer more than twice in a 12-month period.
If a business does not act on a consumer's request it shall inform the consumer without delay of the reasons and of the consumer's rights to appeal.
If requests are manifestly unfounded or excessive, particularly because of their repetitive character, a business may charge a reasonable fee or refuse to act, and the business bears the burden of demonstrating that the request is manifestly unfounded or excessive.
A business that discloses personal information to a service provider, contractor, or third party in compliance with the Act is not liable for the recipient's violations if it did not have actual knowledge or reason to believe the recipient intended to violate the Act.
The Act does not require a business, service provider, or contractor to reidentify information, to retain information it would not otherwise retain, or to maintain information in identifiable or linkable form in order to link verifiable consumer requests to personal information.
The rights afforded to consumers under the Act shall not adversely affect the rights and freedoms of other natural persons, and a verifiable consumer request shall not extend to personal information that belongs to another natural person.
The Act does apply to personal information collected in the course of a person acting as a job applicant, employee, owner, director, officer, medical staff member, or independent contractor, to the extent used solely in that context, with the emergency contact and administration-of-benefits exceptions.
A business that sells or shares personal information or uses sensitive personal information beyond authorized purposes shall provide a clear and conspicuous link on its homepage titled "Do Not Sell or Share My Personal Information" enabling the consumer to opt out, and, where applicable, a link titled "Limit the Use of My Sensitive Personal Information," or may use a single, clearly labeled link for both choices.
A business is not required to provide those links if it allows consumers to opt out by honoring an opt-out preference signal sent with the consumer's consent by a platform, technology, or mechanism indicating the consumer's intent to opt out of the sale or sharing of personal information or to limit sensitive personal information, based on technical specifications set by regulation.
A business that honors opt-out preference signals may provide a link enabling the consumer to consent to the business ignoring the signal for that business, provided revocation is as easy as the affirmative consent and the consent experience meets regulatory specifications and does not employ dark patterns.
A business shall not require a consumer to create an account or provide unnecessary additional information to exercise an opt-out, and shall use information collected in connection with an opt-out request solely to comply with the request.
A business shall ensure that individuals responsible for handling consumer privacy inquiries are informed of the requirements of the Act and how to direct consumers to exercise their rights.
A consumer may authorize another person to act on the consumer's behalf to opt out of the sale or sharing of personal information, and through a verifiable consumer request more generally.
"Personal information" means information that identifies, relates to, describes, is reasonably capable of being associated with, or could reasonably be linked, directly or indirectly, with a particular consumer or household.
Personal information includes identifiers, information described by Civil Code section 1798.80(e), characteristics of protected classifications, commercial information, biometric information, internet activity, geolocation data, audio or visual and similar information, professional or employment-related information, education information, inferences drawn to create a consumer profile, and sensitive personal information.
Personal information does not include publicly available information, lawfully obtained truthful information that is a matter of public concern, deidentified information, or aggregate consumer information, and can exist in physical, digital, and abstract digital formats, including artificial intelligence systems capable of outputting personal information.
"Sensitive personal information" includes social security and similar identifying numbers, account credentials, precise geolocation, racial or ethnic origin, citizenship or immigration status, religious or philosophical beliefs, union membership, the contents of a consumer's mail, email, and text messages where the business is not the intended recipient, genetic data, and neural data.
"Neural data" means information that is generated by measuring the activity of a consumer's central or peripheral nervous system, and that is not inferred from nonneural information.
Sensitive personal information also includes the processing of biometric information to uniquely identify the consumer, personal information concerning health, and personal information concerning a consumer's sex life or sexual orientation.
"Business" means a for-profit entity that does business in California, determines the purposes and means of processing personal information, and satisfies one or more thresholds of annual gross revenues over 25 million dollars, buying, selling, or sharing the personal information of 100,000 or more consumers or households annually, or deriving 50 percent or more of annual revenues from selling or sharing consumers' personal information, and includes commonly branded controlled entities, certain joint ventures, and entities that voluntarily certify compliance.
"Service provider" and "contractor" mean persons that process personal information on behalf of a business for a business purpose under a written contract that prohibits selling or sharing the information and retaining, using, or disclosing it for other purposes, outside the direct business relationship, or combining it with information from other persons, with specified exceptions.
"Third party" means a person who is neither the business with whom the consumer intentionally interacts nor a service provider or contractor.
"Cross-context behavioral advertising" means the targeting of advertising to a consumer based on the consumer's personal information obtained from activity across businesses, websites, applications, or services other than the one with which the consumer intentionally interacts.
"Dark pattern" means a user interface designed or manipulated with the substantial effect of subverting or impairing user autonomy, decisionmaking, or choice.
"Deidentified" means information that cannot reasonably be used to infer information about, or otherwise be linked to, a particular consumer, where the business takes reasonable measures to prevent reassociation, publicly commits to maintain and use it in deidentified form, and contractually obligates recipients to do the same.
"Profiling" means any form of automated processing of personal information to evaluate personal aspects of a natural person, including analyzing or predicting performance at work, economic situation, health, preferences, interests, reliability, behavior, location, or movements.
"Precise geolocation" means data derived from a device used to locate a consumer within a geographic area equal to or less than a circle with a radius of 1,850 feet, except as prescribed by regulations.
The obligations imposed on businesses by the Act shall not restrict a business's ability to comply with federal, state, or local law, with a court order or subpoena, or with a civil, criminal, or regulatory inquiry or investigation.
Law enforcement agencies may direct a business not to delete a consumer's personal information for successive 90-day periods in connection with an agency-approved investigation.
The Act does not apply where compliance would violate an evidentiary privilege, and does not apply to medical information governed by the Confidentiality of Medical Information Act, to protected health information governed by HIPAA, or to clinical trials conducted in accordance with the Common Rule, subject to conditions.
The Act does not apply to activities involving consumer credit reporting information subject to the federal Fair Credit Reporting Act, to personal information collected subject to the Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act, the California Financial Information Privacy Act, or the Farm Credit Act, or to personal information subject to the federal Driver's Privacy Protection Act, provided that these exemptions do not apply to the security breach provision.
The right to opt out of sale or sharing does not apply to vehicle or vessel information and ownership information retained or shared between a dealer and a manufacturer for the purpose of warranty or recall repairs, where the information is not sold, shared, or used for any other purpose.
Deidentified personal information derived from patient information regulated by HIPAA or the Confidentiality of Medical Information Act is exempt, and reidentifying such information is prohibited except for enumerated purposes such as treatment, payment, health care operations, public health activities, and research conducted under applicable ethics rules.
A consumer whose nonencrypted and nonredacted personal information, or whose email address combined with a password or security question and answer, is subject to unauthorized access and exfiltration, theft, or disclosure as a result of a business's failure to implement and maintain reasonable security procedures and practices may institute a civil action for statutory damages of not less than 100 dollars and not greater than 750 dollars per consumer per incident or actual damages, whichever is greater, for injunctive or declaratory relief, and for other relief the court deems proper, with the statutory amounts subject to periodic adjustment.
In assessing statutory damages the court shall consider the nature and seriousness of the misconduct, the number and persistence of violations, their duration, the willfulness of the misconduct, and the defendant's assets, liabilities, and net worth.
Before bringing an action for statutory damages the consumer must give the business 30 days' written notice of the specific provisions violated, and no action for statutory damages may be brought if the business actually cures the violation and provides an express written statement of cure, while no notice is required for an action solely for actual pecuniary damages.
A consumer may bring an action to enforce a business's written statement of cure and seek statutory damages for each subsequent breach of it and for any violation postdating the statement.
The private right of action applies only to the security breach provision and shall not serve as the basis for a private right of action under any other law.
Any business, service provider, contractor, or other person that violates the Act shall be liable for an administrative fine of not more than 2,500 dollars for each violation, or 7,500 dollars for each intentional violation and each violation involving the personal information of consumers the person knows are under 16 years of age, in an administrative enforcement action brought by the California Privacy Protection Agency, with amounts subject to periodic adjustment and deposit in the Consumer Privacy Fund.
Where the agency determines there is probable cause, it shall hold a hearing under the Administrative Procedure Act and may order the violator to cease and desist and to pay the administrative fine to the Consumer Privacy Fund, and persons responsible for the same violation are jointly and severally liable.
The agency may subpoena witnesses, compel testimony, and require production of records, including in exercising its power to audit a business's compliance with the Act.
No administrative action alleging a violation shall be commenced more than five years after the violation, with tolling for fraudulent concealment and for delay in producing documents ordered by a superior court.
The agency may bring a civil action in superior court to collect unpaid administrative fines after exhaustion of judicial review.
The agency and any court shall consider the good faith cooperation of the business, service provider, contractor, or other person in determining the amount of any administrative fine or civil penalty, and a business shall not be required to pay both an administrative fine and a civil penalty for the same violation.
The agency shall adopt regulations governing, among other matters, verification rules for consumer requests, technical specifications for the opt-out preference signal, annual cybersecurity audits for businesses whose processing presents significant risk to security, regular risk assessments for processing that presents significant risk to consumers' privacy with the goal of restricting or prohibiting processing whose risks outweigh its benefits, and access and opt-out rights with respect to automated decisionmaking technology including profiling, with access responses including meaningful information about the logic involved and the likely outcome for the consumer.
Monetary thresholds and amounts under the Act shall be adjusted periodically by the agency to reflect changes in the Consumer Price Index, and the adjustments are exempt from the rulemaking provisions of the Administrative Procedure Act.
Any provision of a contract or agreement that purports to waive or limit a consumer's rights under the Act shall be deemed contrary to public policy and shall be void and unenforceable, without preventing a consumer from declining to exercise rights or from later authorizing a sale or sharing after opting out.
The Act shall be liberally construed to effectuate its purposes, is intended to supplement federal and state law where permissible, and shall not apply where preempted by or in conflict with federal law or the United States or California Constitution.
