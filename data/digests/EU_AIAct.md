The purpose of this Regulation is to improve the functioning of the internal market by laying down a uniform legal framework in particular for the development, the placing on the market, the putting into service and the use of artificial intelligence systems ("AI systems") in the Union, in conformity with Union values, to promote the uptake of human-centric and trustworthy AI while ensuring a high level of protection of health, safety, fundamental rights as enshrined in the Charter of Fundamental Rights, including democracy, the rule of law and environmental protection, against the harmful effects of AI systems, and to support innovation.
The aim of Union rules on AI is to promote the uptake of human-centric and trustworthy AI and to ensure a high level of protection of health, safety, fundamental rights enshrined in the Charter, including democracy, the rule of law and environmental protection, while supporting innovation and improving the functioning of the internal market.
AI systems can be easily deployed in a large variety of economic sectors and many parts of society, including across borders, and it is therefore necessary for Member States to lay down rules on the placing on the market, putting into service and use of AI systems in the Union, thereby promoting the development of a single market for lawful, safe and trustworthy AI applications.
Certain AI systems can have a particularly significant impact on the functioning of democratic societies, and the disrespect of Union values and fundamental rights can result in harm of particularly high intensity and affect an indefinite number of persons.
This Regulation covers all operators placing AI systems on the market or putting them into service in the Union, whether they are public or private.
AI sysmeanvs a machine-based system that is designed to operate with varying levels of autonomy and that may exhibit adaptiveness after deployment, and that, for explicit or implicit objectives, infers, from the input it receives, how to generate outputs such as predictions, content, recommendations, or decisions that can influence physical or virtual environments.
Risk means the combination of the probability of an occurrence of harm and the severity of that harm.
Provider means a natural or legal person, public authority, agency or other body that develops an AI system or a general-purpose AI model or that has an AI system or a general-purpose AI model developed and places it on the market or puts the AI system into service under its own name or trademark.
Deployer means a natural or legal person, public authority, agency or other body using an AI system under its authority except where the AI system is used in the course of a personal non-professional activity.
Impostor means a natural or legal person located or established in the Union which places on the market an AI system that bears the name or trademark of a natural or legal person established in a third country.
Distributor means a natural or legal person in the supply chain, other than the provider or the importer, which makes an AI system available on the Union market.
Operator means a provider, product manufacturer, deployer, authorised representative, importer or distributor.
Biometric data means personal data resulting from specific technical processing relating to the physical, physiological or behavioural characteristics of a natural person, such as facial images or dactyloscopic data.
Biometric identification means the automated recognition of physical, physiological, behavioural or psychological human features for the purpose of establishing the identity of a natural person by comparing biometric data of that individual to biometric data of individuals stored in a database.
General-purpose AI model means an AI model, including where such an AI model is trained with a large amount of data using self-supervision at scale, that displays significant generality and is capable of competently performing a wide range of distinct tasks regardless of the way the model is placed on the market and that can be integrated into a variety of downstream systems or applications, except AI models that are used for research, development or prototyping activities before they are placed on the market.
Systemic risk means a risk that is specific to the high-impact capabilities of general-purpose AI models, having a significant impact on the Union market due to their reach, or due to actual or reasonably foreseeable negative effects on public health, safety, public security, fundamental rights, or the society as a whole, that can be propagated at scale across the value chain.
High-impact capabilities means capabilities that match or exceed the capabilities recorded in the most advanced general-purpose AI models.
Real-time remote biometric identification system means a remote biometric identification system whereby the capturing of biometric data, the comparison and the identification all occur without a significant delay, comprising not only instant identification, but also limited short delays in order to avoid circumvention.
AI literacy means skills, knowledge and understanding that allow providers, deployers and affected persons, taking into account their respective rights and obligations in the context of this Regulation, to make an informed deployment of AI systems, as well as to gain awareness about the opportunities and risks of AI and its possible harm to individuals and society.
Serious incident means an incident or malfunctioning of an AI system that directly or indirectly leads to the death of a person, serious damage to a person's health, a serious and irreversible disruption of the management and operation of critical infrastructure, the infringement of obligations under Union law intended to protect fundamental rights, or serious damage to property or the environment.
This Regulation applies to providers placing on the market or putting into service AI systems or placing on the market general-purpose AI models in the Union, irrespective of whether those providers are established or located within the Union or in a third country, to deployers of AI systems that have their place of establishment or are located within the Union, to providers and deployers of AI systems that have their place of establishment or are located in a third country, and to importers and distributors of AI systems, product manufacturers placing on the market or putting into service an AI system together with their product under their own name or trademark, and authorised representatives of providers.
For AI systems that are a safety component of a product, or that are themselves a product, falling within the scope of certain Union harmonisation legislation listed in Section A of Annex I, only Article 53(1), Article 54, Article 72(4), Article 84(5), Article 89, Article 90, Article 91 and Article 99, with the exception of Article 99(7), point (c), apply.
This Regulation does not apply to AI systems or AI models, including their output, specifically developed and put into service for the sole purpose of scientific research and development, to any research, testing or development activity regarding AI systems or AI models prior to their being placed on the market or put into service, to AI systems specifically developed and put into service for national security, defence and military purposes, to public authorities in a third country or international organisations falling within the scope of this Regulation where used in the framework of international cooperation or agreements for law enforcement and judicial cooperation with the Union or with one or more Member States, or to AI systems released under free and open-source licences unless they are placed on the market or put into service as high-risk AI systems.
The placing on the market, putting into service or use of AI systems that deploy subliminal techniques beyond a person's consciousness or purposefully manipulative or deceptive techniques with the objective or the effect of materially distorting a person's or a group of persons' behaviour by appreciably impairing the person's ability to make an informed decision, thereby causing significant harm, is prohibited.
The placing on the market or putting into service of AI systems that exploit any of the vulnerabilities of a natural person or a specific group of persons due to their age, disability or a specific social or economic situation is prohibited where it has the objective or the effect of materially distorting the behaviour of that person or a person belonging to that group in a manner that causes significant harm.
The placing on the market or putting into service of AI systems for the evaluation or classification of natural persons or groups of persons by social scoring that leads to detrimental or unfavourable treatment of certain natural persons or groups, where it is unjustified or disproportionate to their social behaviour or its gravity, is prohibited.
The use of AI systems for making risk assessments of natural persons based solely on the profiling of a natural person or on assessing their personality traits and characteristics to predict the likelihood of offending, where such assessments are based solely on profiling, is prohibited.
The placing on the market or putting into service of AI systems intended to be used by or on behalf of law enforcement authorities to assess the risk of a natural person to become the victim of criminal offences, based solely on profiling or on assessing their personality traits and characteristics, is prohibited.
The placing on the market or putting into service of AI systems that create or expand facial recognition databases through the untargeted scraping of facial images from the internet or CCTV footage is prohibited.
The placing on the market or putting into service of AI systems to infer emotions of a natural person in areas of workplace and education institutions is prohibited, except where intended to be put in place or into the market for medical or safety reasons.
The placing on the market or putting into service of biometric categorisation systems that categorise individually natural persons based on their biometric data to deduce or infer their race, political opinions, trade union membership, religious or philosophical beliefs, sex life or sexual orientation is prohibited.
The use of real-time remote biometric identification systems in publicly accessible spaces for the purposes of law enforcement is prohibited, unless and in so far as such use is strictly necessary for the targeted search for specific victims of abduction, trafficking in human beings or sexual exploitation, for the prevention of a specific, substantial and imminent threat to the life or physical safety of natural persons or a genuine and present or genuine and foreseeable threat of a terrorist attack, or for the localisation or identification of a person suspected of having committed a criminal offence.
The use of emotionally manipulative AI practices, which exploit vulnerabilities of consumers or customers by detecting emotional distress without reasonable context and using such detection to influence those persons unfairly, and the use of AI systems to categorise persons based on profiling of their personality traits to infer or predict their ethnicity, is prohibited.
An AI system shall be considered to be high-risk where it is intended to be used as a safety component of a product, or is itself such a product, covered by the Union harmonisation legislation listed in Annex I and whose safety component is required to undergo a third-party conformity assessment, or where it is listed in Annex III, unless the system does not pose a significant risk of harm to the health, safety or fundamental rights of natural persons, including by not materially influencing the outcome of decision-making.
High-risk AI systems as referred to in Article 6(2) include AI systems used in biometrics, in so far as their use is permitted under relevant Union or national law, in critical infrastructure, in education and vocational training, in employment, workers management and access to self-employment, in access to and enjoyment of essential private services and essential public services and benefits, in law enforcement, in migration, asylum and border control management, in the administration of justice and democratic processes, and in emotion recognition systems used in the areas of workplace and education institutions for purposes other than medical or safety reasons.
High-risk AI systems shall be designed and developed in such a way as to ensure that their operation is sufficiently transparent to enable deployers to interpret a system's output and use it appropriately, and shall be accompanied by instructions for use in an appropriate digital format or otherwise that include concise, complete, correct and clear information that is relevant, accessible and comprehensible to deployers.
High-risk AI systems shall be designed and developed with capabilities enabling the automatic recording of events ("logs") over the lifetime of the system, in order to ensure a level of traceability of the AI system's functioning appropriate to the intended purpose of the system.
High-risk AI systems shall be designed and developed in such a way that they can be effectively overseen by natural persons during the period in which they are in use, and human oversight shall aim to prevent or minimise the risks to health, safety or fundamental rights that may emerge when a high-risk AI system is used.
High-risk AI systems shall be designed and developed in such a way that they achieve an appropriate level of accuracy, robustness and cybersecurity, and perform consistently in those respects throughout their lifecycle, resilient to attempts by unauthorised third parties to alter their use, outputs or performance by exploiting system vulnerabilities.
Providers of high-risk AI systems shall ensure that the systems undergo the relevant conformity assessment procedure, draw up an EU declaration of conformity, affix the CE marking, register themselves and their systems in the EU database, and, where they are established in a third country, appoint an authorised representative established in the Union.
Providers shall have a quality management system in place which complies with Article 17, and shall keep the technical documentation, the documentation concerning the quality management system, the documentation regarding changes approved by notified bodies, and the certificates issued by notified bodies for a period of at least 10 years after the AI system has been placed on the market or put into service.
Importers shall ensure that the high-risk AI system is accompanied by the instructions for use, the EU declaration of conformity, and the CE marking, and shall indicate on the high-risk AI system their name, registered trade name or registered trademark, the address at which the provider can be contacted by the supervisory authority, and ensure the storage or transport conditions do not jeopardise its conformity.
Distributors shall verify that the high-risk AI system has been accompanied by the required documentation, bears the required markings, and that the provider and the importer have complied with their obligations, before making it available on the market.
Deployers of high-risk AI systems shall take appropriate technical and organisational measures to ensure they use such systems in accordance with the instructions for use, shall assign human oversight to natural persons who have the necessary competence, training and authority, shall ensure that the input data is relevant and sufficiently representative in view of the intended purpose of the high-risk AI system, shall monitor the operation, shall inform the provider when the system presents a risk, shall retain logs, shall keep logs automatically generated under their control for a period of at least six months, shall inform workers and their representatives before putting into service, and shall conduct a fundamental rights impact assessment before deploying.
Before putting into service or using a high-risk AI system, deployers who are bodies governed by public law, or are private entities providing public services, and deployers of high-risk AI systems referred to in points 5(b) and (c) of Annex III shall perform an assessment of the impact on fundamental rights that the use of such system may produce, including the deployer's specific context of use, the categories of natural persons and groups likely to be affected, the specific risks of harm likely to have an impact on those categories, the possible negative impact on fundamental rights, and the measures to be taken in the case of the materialisation of those risks.
Providers of AI systems, shall ensure that AI systems intended to interact directly with natural persons are designed and developed in such a way that the natural persons concerned are informed that they are interacting with an AI system, unless this is obvious from the circumstances and the context of use.
Providers of AI systems, including GPAI systems, generating synthetic audio, image, video or text content, shall ensure that the outputs of the AI system are marked in a machine-readable format and detectable as artificially generated or manipulated.
Deployers of an emotion recognition system or a biometric categorisation system shall inform of the operation of the system the natural persons exposed thereto and process the personal data in accordance with Regulation (EU) 2016/679, Regulation (EU) 2018/1725 and Directive (EU) 2016/680, as applicable.
Deployers of an AI system that generates or manipulates image, audio or video content constituting a deep fake shall disclose that the content has been artificially generated or manipulated.
The Regulation provides for general-purpose AI models which shall be subject to specific obligations where they have systemic risk, and where a general-purpose AI model meets the condition referred to in Article 51(1), point (a), the provider shall notify the relevant national authority without delay, with the possibility of demonstrating sufficient guarantee that the model does not present systemic risk.
Providers of general-purpose AI models shall draw up technical documentation, draw up documentation and information for providers who intend to integrate the general-purpose AI model into their AI system, put in place a policy to comply with Union copyright law, and draw up and make publicly available a sufficiently detailed summary about the content used for training of the general-purpose AI model.
Providers of general-purpose AI models with systemic risk shall, in addition, perform model evaluation, assess and mitigate possible systemic risks, report serious incidents, and ensure an adequate level of cybersecurity protection.
The AI Office shall develop Union capabilities and expertise in AI, monitor and supervise the implementation and application of this Regulation, in particular with regard to general-purpose AI models, and contribute to the various tasks of the Commission and the Board under this Regulation.
Providers of general-purpose AI models placing on the market or putting into service their models in the Union shall appoint an authorised representative established in the Union.
The Commission shall ensure uniform and consistent application of this Regulation and contribute to the effective cooperation between the Member States and the coordination between the authorities of the Member States, and the AI Office shall develop Union expertise and capabilities in the field of AI.
Providers of general-purpose AI models shall comply with the obligations laid down in Article 53 by 2 August 2025, and with the obligations for general-purpose AI models placed on the market before 2 August 2025 within two years.
The Board shall advise and assist the Commission and the Member States in order to facilitate the consistent and effective application of this Regulation, and shall consist of one representative per Member State, with the European Data Protection Supervisor participating as an observer, and shall be established by 2 August 2025.
The AI Office shall develop a monitoring and evaluation framework regarding the operation of this Regulation, carry out evaluations and reviews, and the Commission shall submit a report on the first evaluation and review to the European Parliament and the Council by 2 August 2029 and every three years thereafter.
Providers of general-purpose AI models shall be permitted to rely on codes of practice to demonstrate compliance with the obligations of Article 53 until harmonised standards are published, and the AI Office may invite providers, national authorities and other relevant stakeholders to participate in the drawing up of these codes.
The powers of the AI Office include carrying out evaluations and reviews of general-purpose AI models, requesting information where necessary, taking measures including requests for information and access to the model, and the Commission may adopt implementing acts setting out the arrangements for a structured dialogue, and the confidentiality of information obtained pursuant to powers conferred under this Regulation shall be respected.
When an AI system or model placed on the market or put into service before 2 August 2026 is no longer compliant with this Regulation, it shall be withdrawn from the market, disabled or recalled, and the hierarchy of measures shall be applied by the relevant market surveillance authorities.
Providers shall establish and document a post-market monitoring system in a manner proportionate to the nature of the AI system and shall proactively collect and review experience gained from the use of the AI system, and the Commission shall establish a single reporting portal by 2 August 2025 for the reporting of serious incidents.
Deployers of high-risk AI systems referred to in Article 27 shall report serious incidents to the relevant market surveillance authority and to the provider without undue delay, and providers shall report any serious incident to the market surveillance authorities of the Member States where that incident occurred immediately after the provider becomes aware of it and no later than 15 days after the serious incident.
For general-purpose AI models with systemic risk, providers shall report serious incidents to the AI Office and, where appropriate, to national competent authorities, and the AI Office shall investigate the causal link between the serious incident and the general-purpose AI model.
Member States shall report to the Commission on an annual basis aggregated information concerning the implementation of this Regulation, including the number of notified bodies, the number of certificates issued and withdrawn, and the number of decisions taken by market surveillance authorities.
The use of this Regulation may be applied, where requested by the Commission, to the extent necessary for the purpose of carrying out the tasks conferred on the Commission by Regulation (EU) 2022/1925 (the Digital Markets Act).
The Commission shall adopt implementing acts to specify the details on the establishment and maintenance of the EU database, and information shall be kept in the central repository concerning the registration of high-risk AI systems by providers and, where applicable, by deployers.
The pooling of resources and capabilities necessary for the enforcement of this Regulation may be used by the Commission and Member States to set up and support an interoperable platform for the sharing of information among authorities.
This Regulation shall apply without prejudice to the Charter, the Union secondary law on data protection and privacy, and Union consumer protection law.
This Regulation harmonises rules for the placing on the market, putting into service and use of AI systems, prohibitions of certain AI practices, specific requirements for high-risk AI systems and obligations for the operators of such systems, harmonised transparency rules for certain AI systems, harmonised rules for the placing on the market of general-purpose AI models, and rules on market monitoring, market surveillance, governance and enforcement.
The AI Office shall be established by 12 February 2024 and shall be part of the Commission, and the AI Office shall monitor the effective implementation and compliance of the Regulation.
The Board shall advise and assist the Commission and the Member States, shall be established by 2 August 2025, and shall draw up its rules of procedure at its first meeting, which shall enter into force following their approval by the Commission.
The Commission shall adopt implementing acts laying down a template for the summary of the training content and rules for the exercise of powers conferred on the AI Office for the supervision and enforcement of the obligations of general-purpose AI models, and shall adopt delegated acts in order to amend the thresholds for general-purpose AI models with systemic risk laid down in Article 51.
Member States shall lay down the rules on penalties and other enforcement measures applicable to infringements of this Regulation, and shall take all measures necessary to ensure that they are properly and effectively implemented, taking into account the guidelines issued by the Commission, including administrative fines of up to 35 000 000 EUR or up to 7 % of total worldwide annual turnover for non-compliance with the prohibition of AI practices, and administrative fines of up to 15 000 000 EUR or up to 3 % of total worldwide annual turnover for non-compliance with other provisions, including obligations of providers and deployers, with the lower cap applying where the offender is an SME.
The Commission shall by 2 August 2025 adopt implementing acts laying down detailed procedural rules for the proceedings of the AI Office in enforcement action against providers of general-purpose AI models, and for the coordinated market surveillance of general-purpose AI systems.
The Commission shall develop the Union's capabilities and expertise relating to AI through the AI Office and shall strengthen cooperation with stakeholders and third countries, and the Commission's AI Office shall be empowered to act independently in the performance of its tasks.
The Regulation is subject to application 20 days after its publication, and its provisions shall apply progressively: Chapters I and II from 2 February 2025, Chapter III Section 4, Chapter V, Chapter VII and Chapter XII and Article 78 from 2 August 2025, with the exception of Article 101 which shall apply from 2 August 2028, and all remaining parts from 2 August 2026, while obligations concerning high-risk AI systems that have been placed on the market or put into service before 2 August 2026 shall apply only if, from that date, those systems are subject to substantial changes in their design, and each of the dates is preceded by appropriate transitional arrangements.
